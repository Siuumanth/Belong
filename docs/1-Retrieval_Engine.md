## 1. Executive Summary

We created a **10-user population test** set in **Bangalore, India** to evaluate how effectively our Stage 1 Candidate Retrieval Engine finds and ranks potential dating matches.

Instead of running slow and expensive AI text analysis for every single pair upfront, **Stage 1** uses smart database filtering and vector embedding similarity to instantly retrieve and rank candidates in order of compatibility.

### Key Finding
**The system works exceptionally well.** It correctly placed the ideal complementary couples as mutual **#1 Top Matches** (`Alice <-> Bob` and `Elena <-> Felix`), and arranged fallback candidates in a logical, descending order of quality.

## 3. How Belong Fetches & Matches Candidates (Simple Breakdown)

Our engine retrieves matches in **2 quick steps**:

```
[ Step 1: SQL Hard Filters ]  --->  [ Step 2: Bidirectional Vector Matching ]  --->  [ Ranked Shortlist ]
(Distance <= 50km, Age, Gender)    (Forward Sim + Reverse Sim = Combined Score)     (#1 Top, #2, #3...)
```

### Step 1: Database Hard Filters
Before comparing personalities, PostgreSQL instantly filters out impossible matches:
- **Location Radius**: Only users living within $50\text{km}$ of each other.
- **Mutual Gender Preference**: Straight males only see straight females, and straight females only see straight males.
- **Mutual Age Limits**: $A$'s age must fit $B$'s age range **AND** $B$'s age must fit $A$'s age range.

### Step 2: Bidirectional Vector Matching (Forward & Reverse Sim)
For candidates that pass the hard filters, we compare two 1536-dimensional AI vector embeddings:

1. **Forward Similarity ($A.\text{wants} \rightarrow B.\text{self}$)**:
   - *"Does Candidate B have what User A is looking for?"*
   - Compares User A's `wants` vector against Candidate B's `self` vector.
2. **Reverse Similarity ($B.\text{wants} \rightarrow A.\text{self}$)**:
   - *"Does User A have what Candidate B is looking for?"*
   - Compares Candidate B's `wants` vector against User A's `self` vector.
3. **Combined Score**:
   $$\text{Combined Score} = 0.5 \times \text{Forward Sim} + 0.5 \times \text{Reverse Sim}$$

> **Why both matter**: Matching cannot be one-sided. Belong only ranks a candidate high if **both people mutually satisfy what the other person is seeking!**

## 5. Notes on Candidate Ordering & Fallback Quality

A crucial question is: **If a user swipes past or passes on Candidate #1, is Candidate #2 actually the next best match?**

### Evaluation of Candidate Order (Rank #1 $\rightarrow$ #2 $\rightarrow$ #3 $\rightarrow$ #4 $\rightarrow$ #5 $\rightarrow$ #6)

#### Alice's Complete Candidate Shortlist:
1. **Rank #1: `Bob` (Score: 0.2302)** — **Best Match**: Perfect alignment on emotional reassurance, stability, plant-based/health habits.
2. **Rank #2: `Quinn` (Score: 0.2020)** — **Next Best Fallback**: Quinn is a tech lead who runs in Cubbon Park and brews filter coffee. Shares Alice's morning routine, just slightly less explicit on verbal reassurance than Bob.
3. **Rank #3: `Ian` (Score: 0.1732)** — **3rd Choice**: Active boulderer. Good fitness match, but his intense conflict style (*"talk right now"*) makes him less compatible than Bob or Quinn.
4. **Rank #4: `Frank` (Score: 0.0904)** — **4th Choice**: Night owl bartender. Schedule and lifestyle clash heavily with Alice's 6 AM yoga.
5. **Rank #5: `Oliver` (Score: 0.0000)** — **Bottom Match**: Low effort/vague persona (*"watch TV, bad vibes"*).

### Key Ordering Rules Confirmed
1. **Strict Monotonicity**: Higher compatibility candidates consistently rank above lower compatibility candidates.
2. **Logical Fallback**: If a user rejects #1, Candidate #2 is legitimately the next best natural alternative in terms of lifestyle and values.
3. **Low-Quality Penalization**: Vague personas (`Oliver`) and heavy lifestyle clashes (`Frank`) are pushed down to the very bottom of the list (#5/#6).
4. **Stage 1 / Stage 2 Synergy**: Stage 1 vector recall retrieves candidates based on overall lifestyle similarity (e.g. `Eve` and `Frank` both like East Bangalore nightlife). Stage 2 LLM reasoning then inspects explicit dealbreakers (Eve's non-smoking rule) and applies disqualification penalties.
